SEO Keyword Research & Recommendations

Coming out of the May 11 check-in: Roberta and Jaylene searched "native ministries" and LIM didn't appear at all. This is why — and the keywords, content, and partnership moves that will actually get LIM found.

Prepared: June 8, 2026 · For: Jaylene, Roberta, Robert

The Core Finding

Good news: LIM already ranks on page one for its real strength — trauma healing. A search for "healing the wounds of trauma Native American Christian" surfaces LIM's own page. That's the winnable lane.

The gap: the broad term "native ministries" is owned by sites with the exact words in their domain — nativemi.org, nativeministry.org — plus big denominational pages and directories. Google reads those domains as the literal answer. We will not out-rank them quickly by adding a keyword or two; that takes content + links over months.

The play: win the high-intent "trauma healing / healing group" searches now, get listed in the Native-ministry directories (which also delivers Robert's partnership/link-sharing goal), and build content toward the head terms over time.

About the numbers: exact monthly search volumes require a paid tool (Google Keyword Planner with an active Ads account, Ahrefs, or Semrush). The Volume column below is a relative estimate (High / Med / Low) from the live search landscape, not hard figures. Before investing heavily in any single term, confirm its volume in one of those tools — happy to set that up.

On-Page Changes for the New Site

Where to weave the Tier-1 terms in. Biggest lever first: page titles and the H1, which are currently brand-only and use none of the words people search.

Home — page title
Now: Lutheran Indian Ministries — Where Faith Meets Tradition
Suggest: Lutheran Indian Ministries — Native-Led Trauma Healing & Gospel Ministry
Home — meta description
Now: already solid (Native-led, Gospel, healing trauma, 574 tribal communities).
Tweak: lead with "Native American & Alaska Native trauma healing groups" so the high-intent words appear in the snippet.
Healing Groups — title & H1
Now: focused on "sessions" and program internals.
Suggest title: Native American Trauma Healing Groups — In-Person & Online | LIM. Work "Alaska Native" and "Fairbanks" into the section copy for the local lane.
New pillar page (recommended)
Build: a "What Is Native American Trauma Healing?" explainer that targets the Tier-1 head phrase, links out to Healing Groups, the curriculum, and facilitator training, and answers the "people also ask" questions. This is the page most likely to climb for the trauma cluster.
Every page — basics
Unique <title> + <meta description> per page, one <h1>, descriptive alt text on images, and a sitemap.xml submitted to Google Search Console at launch.

Partnerships & Backlinks

This is the single biggest off-page lever — and it doubles as the partner outreach Robert keeps asking for. Getting listed in Native-ministry directories and swapping links with peer orgs tells Google LIM belongs in this category, and puts LIM in front of communities already doing ministry.

  • Directories to get listed in — nativemi.org (Native Ministries International — explicitly a directory of native tribes & ministries) and chief.org's "Network of Native Ministries." A listing = a quality backlink + discovery.
  • Reciprocal links with peer orgs — reach out to swap links / share resources with: North American Indigenous Missions (NAIM), The Red Road, Cru "Nations," Church of God / PCG Native American Ministries, American Baptist Home Mission Societies, and Alaska peers (InterAct Ministries, GraceWorks Alaska, Diocese of Fairbanks Native Ministry, Alaska Mission for Christ).
  • Curriculum credibility link — ask the Trauma Healing Institute (American Bible Society) / World Impact to list LIM as a Native-context partner using their model. THI already shows up for the facilitator-training searches.
  • The outreach ask — exactly as discussed May 11: "We're giving this healing-group model away free — would you share links and point your communities to it?" Strong reciprocity story.
