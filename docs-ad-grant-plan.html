How LIM uses $10,000 a month of free Google search ads

Put LIM at the top of Google when people search for trauma healing, for Native ministry, and for the scripture LIM already writes about. Then turn those visits into registrations, applications and supporters.

This plan works the same whoever runs the account: LIM itself, Gary, Ad Grants Pilot or an agency. It is the brief they work from. It sets out what to advertise, to whom, with what words, where each click lands, and how we'll know it worked.

The grant's rules shape the plan

The grant pays for text ads on Google Search only (no YouTube, no display banners). Google enforces rules to keep it, and every choice below is built to pass them. Confirm the current wording when we enroll, because Google updates them.

RuleWhat it means for LIM
Keep at least a 5% click rate across the accountOnly bid on searches that clearly match what LIM offers. A brand campaign (people searching "Lutheran Indian Ministries") lifts the average.
No single-word or overly broad keywords"Trauma" or "ministry" alone is out. Every keyword is a phrase that shows intent.
Track real conversionsRegistrations, applications, sign-ups and gifts must be counted in Google. This depends on the cookie-notice decision on today's agenda.
Each campaign needs at least two ad groups, two ads each, and sitelinksBuilt into every campaign below.
Bids cap at $2 a click unless Google's automatic "maximize conversions" bidding is onTurn that bidding on once conversions are tracked, so LIM can win the competitive searches.
Answer Google's yearly program surveyJaylene, once a year.

Six campaigns

Ordered by when they start. Keywords are shown as people type them. In the account each one is set to phrase or exact match, so ads only appear on searches that mean the same thing. The sample ads are written to Google's limits (headlines up to 30 characters, descriptions up to 90). Google mixes and matches them, so each campaign also gets a pool of extra lines.

How we'll know it's working

  • Goals Google counts: healing-group registration, Tree of Life application, training sign-up, newsletter sign-up, Bible-in-a-Year request, and (after the fix) a completed gift.
  • Reported at every check-in: clicks, click rate (must stay above 5%), cost the grant covered, and registrations and sign-ups by campaign. One short table in the agenda.
  • The cookie notice matters here. Google can only count a goal for visitors whose tracking loads. With today's Accept-only banner it sees about 1 visitor in 9, so the grant would look like it isn't working even when it is. That's the first decision on today's agenda.
  • Fair test: after 90 days, compare registrations and sign-ups per month with the 90 days before. Keep what works, cut what doesn't.

Who runs it

Google requires active management. Whoever runs it works from this plan, so the choice is about upkeep, not strategy.

  • The rules are what lose grants, so daily watching matters most. Accounts are suspended for falling under the 5% click rate or carrying low-quality keywords. Those problems build up over days. Software that checks daily catches them. A monthly human review often doesn't.
  • Half the agency price, with LIM's voice kept. The software handles the routine work: bids, pausing weak keywords, the compliance checks. Gary's hour a month covers what software can't judge: whether the ads sound like LIM, whether each click lands on the right page, and adding a new ad group whenever a healing group is announced.
  • The free month lines up with Fairbanks. Starting this week, the 30-day trial covers the run-up to Nov 11–13. LIM will see search registrations for a real session before paying anything.
  • It saves the prepaid hours for the website. Running the account by hand would draw 3–4 hours a month from the maintenance block, about $2,500–3,400 a year at the block rate. That's more than the software costs, and those hours are better spent on devotions, forms and pages.
  • Nothing is locked in. The Google Ads account belongs to LIM, and this plan is written for any manager. If the grant grows into a major channel, moving to an agency like Nonprofit Megaphone keeps the account, its history and the plan.
  • To check during the trial: how cancellation works, and that LIM keeps admin access to its own account. Ask before the trial ends.
