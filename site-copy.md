### Healing The Wounds Of Trauma

- `[idx-037]` **H2** — Healing the Wounds of Trauma
- `[idx-038]` **Paragraph** — A Bible-based small group program led by Native facilitators and supported by prayer warriors. Open to everyone 18 and older. No faith background needed.
- `[idx-039]` **Paragraph** — No pressure, no force. You come as you are and leave when the time is right. The Holy Spirit does the heavy lifting.
- `[idx-040]` **Paragraph** — I got to let go of hurts and disappointments that I've experienced in my entire life. And talk about freedom. Oh, the freedom. I have a hard time putting into words how you feel so much lighter — how you feel so not shackled anymore.
- `[idx-041]` **Paragraph** — — Roberta (Kitigruak), Ministry Coordinator & Healing Group Participant Anchorage, Alaska
- `[idx-042]` **H3** — What Happens in a Healing Group?
- `[idx-043]` **Paragraph** — Six core sessions over 16 hours walk participants through scripture and personal reflection. Each session builds on the last.
- `[idx-044]` **Button** — 1 What Is a Wound of the Heart?
- `[idx-045]` **Paragraph** — Heart wounds can begin in childhood, young adulthood, or today. This session helps participants name and understand their wounds — from abuse and loss to historical trauma. Naming the wound is the first step toward healing it.
- `[idx-046]` **Button** — 2 What Can Help Our Heart Wounds Heal?
- `[idx-047]` **Paragraph** — Practical, Bible-based tools for the healing journey. Participants discover that healing is supported by community, prayer, and God's Word. Exercises move learning from the head to the heart — where lasting change happens.
- `[idx-048]` **Button** — 3 What Happens When Someone Is Grieving?
- `[idx-049]` **Paragraph** — A compassionate look at what grief looks like — and where people get stuck. When Roberta learned that Jesus wept , it gave her permission to feel. Understanding grief — and knowing you don't have to stay stuck — is deeply freeing.
- `[idx-050]` **Button** — 4 If God Loves Us, Why Do We Suffer?
- `[idx-051]` **Paragraph** — Perhaps the most honest question we carry. This session doesn't avoid it — scripture shows that God is present in suffering, not absent from it. Your pain is not a sign God has left you. He weeps with you.
- `[idx-052]` **Button** — 5 Bringing Our Pain to the Cross & Forgiving Others
- `[idx-053]` **Paragraph** — One of the most powerful — and most difficult — sessions. Participants are gently led through releasing pain and beginning the journey of forgiving others. There is no force, no timeline. You go only where you are ready to go, supported by the community around you.
- `[idx-054]` **Button** — 6 Looking Back, Looking Forward — Closing with Hope
- `[idx-055]` **Paragraph** — The closing session brings everything together — looking back at the journey and forward with hope. Participants also begin seeing how they can share this with their own communities. The healing group is not a one-and-done; it's the beginning of a lifelong journey.
- `[idx-056]` **Paragraph** — Over 390 scriptures woven throughout all six sessions using the Restoring Hope Bible.
- `[idx-057]` **Button** — ✦ What Is a Prayer Warrior? How Is Prayer Woven Into Every Session?
- `[idx-058]` **Paragraph** — A Prayer Warrior has been through the healing group. They want to support others — not as a facilitator, but through prayer. They pray before, during, and after every session — for every participant, those who couldn't make it, and the facilitators.
- `[idx-059]` **Paragraph** — No one is missed. Prayer Warriors are also available one-on-one: if someone needs to talk outside the group, a Prayer Warrior is there. Every healing group is covered in prayer before it begins.
- `[idx-060]` **H3** — Ready to Begin?
- `[idx-061]` **Paragraph** — Sessions held on reservations, in villages, in cities. In-person and online. Lunch provided at in-person sessions. All are welcome — no faith background required.

### What To Expect In Each Session

- `[hgs-080]` **H2** — The Six Core Sessions
- `[hgs-081]` **Paragraph** — Each session builds on the last. Over 390 scriptures are woven throughout, using the Restoring Hope Bible. You go only where you're ready to go.
- `[hgs-082]` **Button** — 1 What Is a Wound of the Heart?
- `[hgs-083]` **Paragraph** — Heart wounds can begin in childhood, young adulthood, or today. This session helps participants name and understand their specific wounds — whether from abuse, loss, historical trauma, or grief. Naming the wound is the first step toward healing it.
- `[hgs-084]` **Button** — 2 What Can Help Our Heart Wounds Heal?
- `[hgs-085]` **Paragraph** — Practical tools for the healing journey — exercises that move understanding from head to heart. Participants discover that healing is a process supported by community, prayer, and scripture. This session also addresses how stress and trauma are held in the body.
- `[hgs-086]` **Button** — 3 What Happens When Someone Is Grieving?
- `[hgs-087]` **Paragraph** — A compassionate look at what grief actually looks like — and where people get stuck. Understanding the grief journey is often cathartic and transforming. Participants learn they don't have to stay stuck, and that tears are cleansing, not weakness.
- `[hgs-088]` **Button** — 4 If God Loves Us, Why Do We Suffer?
- `[hgs-089]` **Paragraph** — The most honest question we carry. Scripture reveals that God is present in suffering — not absent from it. Participants discover that pain is not a sign of abandonment. We learn that Jesus wept — and that changes everything.
- `[hgs-090]` **Button** — 5 Bringing Our Pain to the Cross & Forgiving Others
- `[hgs-091]` **Paragraph** — One of the most powerful sessions. Participants are gently led through releasing pain and beginning the journey of forgiving others. No pressure, no timeline. You go only where you're ready. The community around you holds space with grace and love.
- `[hgs-092]` **Button** — 6 Looking Back, Looking Forward — Closing with Hope
- `[hgs-093]` **Paragraph** — The closing session brings everything together — looking back at the healing journey, looking forward with hope. Participants also discover how they can share this with others. The healing group is the beginning, not the end.

### Article Content

- `[bph-006]` **Paragraph** — "I can do all things through him who strengthens me."
- `[bph-007]` **Paragraph** — If you are like me, you can recite this verse — but in real life, find yourself far from being able "to do all things."
- `[bph-008]` **Paragraph** — Why? Because we do not let Christ strengthen us? Every Biblically-aware believer knows "what God wants," and they seek to do it with His strength. Or do we know this verse so well that it has lost its impact on how we "work out our salvation"?
- `[bph-009]` **Paragraph** — "Work out your own salvation with fear and trembling."
- `[bph-010]` **Paragraph** — It is easy for a familiar verse to become invisible — recited without being received. Philippians 4:13 is one of those verses. We put it on T-shirts and phone cases. We quote it before big moments. But Paul wrote it from prison, not from a platform. He wasn't claiming supernatural athletic performance. He was describing something quieter and harder: the ability to be content in every circumstance, sustained not by his own resolve, but by Someone greater moving through him.
- `[bph-011]` **Paragraph** — The question isn't whether we know the verse. The question is whether we are actually living in the dependency it describes.
- `[bph-012]` **Paragraph** — "Years of bondage were broken and this person was set free — free indeed."
- `[bph-013]` **Paragraph** — Recently, a close friend of mine discovered this truth regarding victory over alcohol, which he had struggled with for years. It was a time of deliverance preceded by many hours of fervent prayer by his family. I was fortunate to be there during his time of deliverance. I remember leaving the prayer time and thinking that something powerful had just taken place.
- `[bph-014]` **Paragraph** — The day after, my friend attended church and was touched by the pastor's closing prayer. More prayers, more pastors — something big happened. Years of bondage were broken and this person was set free — free indeed. The intercessions of others had come to God's throne of grace and were answered.
- `[bph-015]` **Paragraph** — Bear one another's burdens — Galatians 6:2
- `[bph-016]` **Paragraph** — Sometimes, Christ's strength is not something we summon alone. It arrives through the hands and voices of the people who carry us when we cannot carry ourselves. My friend did not find freedom through willpower. He found it through a community that refused to stop praying.
- `[bph-017]` **Paragraph** — "Bear one another's burdens, and so fulfill the law of Christ."
- `[bph-018]` **Paragraph** — We are to carry one another's burdens. This is not a suggestion — it is the fulfillment of the law of Christ. And prayer is the primary tool for doing it. Not prayer as ritual, but prayer as warfare. Prayer as love made active.
- `[bph-019]` **Paragraph** — "We destroy arguments and every lofty opinion raised against the knowledge of God, and take every thought captive to obey Christ."
- `[bph-020]` **Paragraph** — Prayer is God's gift, made possible by the mediating sacrifice of Jesus. It is the tool by which our thoughts — so prone to wander, to despair, to rehearse old wounds — are brought captive to the mind of God. When we pray for someone, we are not just speaking words into the air. We are positioning ourselves, and them, before the throne of grace.
- `[bph-021]` **Paragraph** — So the question comes back: I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me. Are you letting Him? And are you part of the community that strengthens others — not through advice or fixing, but through the faithful, unglamorous work of prayer?
- `[bph-022]` **Paragraph** — Jesus, Thank you for removing the barrier between us and God through Your perfect sacrifice on the cross. Thank you that your strength is not reserved for the strong — that it comes to us precisely in our weakness, through the prayers of those who love us and the Spirit who intercedes when we have no words left. Teach us to stop performing Philippians 4:13 and to start living it — in dependence on You, and in faithful care for one another.

- `[bch-004]` **H1** — The Choice: Native OR Christian?
- `[bch-005]` **Paragraph** — For more than 500 years, Native Americans have been told to choose. LIM exists to answer that false ultimatum — once and for all.
- `[bch-006]` **Paragraph** — By Tim Young Eagle, CFRE (Pawnee) · Executive Director, Lutheran Indian Ministries


### Article Content

- `[bch-007]` **Paragraph** — The letter arrived handwritten on lined notebook paper — the kind with three holes punched along the side and a red margin that you might have used for a high school composition assignment once upon a time. The return address was from a correctional facility out east.
- `[bch-008]` **Paragraph** — We occasionally receive mail from Native American inmates. Usually, the writer tells us his life's story or about his faith journey. But this man was asking for advice on a question that cut deep.
- `[bch-009]` **Paragraph** — "I am registered as an American Indian, but my religion is listed as Lutheran. If I want to participate in the Native American activities or classes, I have to change my religion. But if I change my religion, I can't participate in the Sunday worship or Bible study."
- `[bch-010]` **Paragraph** — "How can I be Lutheran and Native?"
- `[bch-011]` **Paragraph** — John closed with a question that has echoed across Native communities for generations. And he asked it from a prison cell — where, stripped of nearly everything else, he was still being told to choose between who he was and who God made him to be.
- `[bch-012]` **H3** — A 500-Year-Old Lie
- `[bch-013]` **Paragraph** — Sadly, John's question is one we are asked often in ministry among Native people. For more than 500 years, Native Americans were told they cannot be both Christian and Native. The Native culture, according to early missionaries, did not fit the Christian religion — and belief in the Gospel required them to give up who they were in order to become something they could never truly be.
- `[bch-014]` **Paragraph** — It wasn't just theology. It was policy. It was enforced. Boarding schools — funded by the federal government and operated by churches — removed Native children from their families, cut their hair, banned their languages, and punished them for singing their songs or speaking their names. The logic was explicit.
- `[bch-015]` **Quote** — "A great general has said that the only good Indian is a dead one... In a sense, I agree with the sentiment, but only in this: that all the Indian there is in the race should be dead. Kill the Indian in him, and save the man."
- `[bch-016]` **Paragraph** — Only once the Indian part of a man had been purged, the thinking went, could the saving grace of Jesus' death and resurrection become available to him. Culture was the barrier. Tradition was the enemy. Identity was the price of admission.
- `[bch-017]` **Paragraph** — This is a lie — a theological error with devastating real-world consequences. And it is still alive. It has been so thoroughly embedded in some Native communities that it is treated as obvious truth: if you follow Jesus, you leave your people behind.
- `[bch-018]` **H3** — What the Gospel Actually Says
- `[bch-019]` **Paragraph** — Lutheran Indian Ministries, through your prayers and gifts, works every day to proclaim the truth that dispels this aged, false teaching. The Gospel of Jesus Christ does not demand cultural death as the price of salvation. It never has. What early missionaries called "pagan culture" — the drum, the dance, the language, the land — was not the enemy of the Gospel. It was the vessel through which God could speak to Native hearts in Native ways.
- `[bch-020]` **Paragraph** — "God speaks to Native hearts in Native ways. You do not have to stop being who you are to receive what He offers."
- `[bch-021]` **Paragraph** — The Gospel has always crossed cultures by entering them — not erasing them. Paul became "all things to all people" not to erase their humanity, but to reach them within it. The Spirit at Pentecost did not force every listener to hear in one language. Every person heard in their own tongue. The miracle was not uniformity. It was recognition.
- `[bch-022]` **H3** — The Man With the Drum
- `[bch-023]` **Paragraph** — For the first time in his life, he felt free.
- `[bch-024]` **Paragraph** — A friend of LIM was asked recently to take part in a Lutheran church service. He agreed — but standing backstage, drum in hand, waiting to process into the sanctuary, the old question rose in him: How can I be Native and Christian? How will this congregation react to our drumming and regalia? How can I be one of them and still be me?
- `[bch-025]` **Paragraph** — He had been invited, with a group of local Native people, to share their faith and their stories — their drumming and their culture. And yet, he felt anxious. Decades of being told to choose had left a wound that didn't close easily.
- `[bch-026]` **Paragraph** — The pastor made the announcement. The drumbeat echoed through the sanctuary.
- `[bch-027]` **Paragraph** — In a solemn, humble, and worshipful movement, they entered — honoring and praising their Savior, Jesus Christ, in the way they were taught by their ancestors. They honored the traditions of their people while worshipping their Holy Father from their innermost being, with all their heart and all their mind and all their soul.
- `[bch-028]` **Paragraph** — "As he walked down the aisle, tears streamed down his face. The Holy Spirit guided him on and his heart softened. For the first time in his life, he felt free. "
- `[bch-029]` **Paragraph** — Free to be Native and to be Christian. Free to praise God with drumming and dancing. Free to be who God had created him to be. At that moment, he knew and understood that Jesus Christ suffered, died, and was resurrected for his salvation — and he gave thanks to God.
- `[bch-030]` **Paragraph** — "Let them praise his name with dancing and make music to him with timbrel and harp. For the Lord takes delight in his people; he crowns the humble with salvation."
- `[bch-031]` **Paragraph** — It is in these moments we can all rejoice and thank God that He sent His Son, Jesus, for the salvation of all people. Not all people who look a certain way. Not all people who conform to a certain culture. All. The drum was always welcome in the house of the Lord. It just took the courage of one man, and the prayers of a whole community, to prove it.
- `[bch-032]` **H3** — John's Answer
- `[bch-033]` **Paragraph** — This is the message we returned to John — and the message we share daily with the Native American, Alaska Native, and Native Hawaiian people we encounter in this ministry:
- `[bch-034]` **Paragraph** — "I know God is with me, and I worship Him where I am. You and LIM are in my prayers. Continue to do God's work and spread His word to all Nations!"
- `[bch-035]` **Paragraph** — From a prison cell, with almost nothing — John had already found the answer. He was worshipping. He was praying for others. He was trusting God to hold both parts of his identity together. The system around him had not caught up to the Gospel. But John had.
- `[bch-036]` **Paragraph** — The letter arrived handwritten on lined notebook paper with three holes punched along the side.
- `[bch-037]` **Paragraph** — Your gift enables us to proclaim the truth Native people need to hear: Jesus died for YOU just as you are. Not as someone else. Not after you've erased your culture, your language, your traditions. Just as you are — Native, whole, and beloved by God.
