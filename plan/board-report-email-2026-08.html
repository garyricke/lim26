Suggested subject: The new LIM website — what changed, and what the numbers told us

Board members,

LIM's website moved off Squarespace and went live on its own platform this month. Here is why that was necessary, what nineteen months of traffic data revealed, and what to watch next. The fuller version, with the tables, is here: lutheranindianministries.org/board-report

Why we left Squarespace

  • Nothing on the site could do real work. It could publish pages, but it could not run an application, gate a form, take a registration into a reviewable queue, or connect our giving form to our advertising. Each of those needed a separate paid service bolted on.
  • We were paying for tools to work around the tool we were paying for. The Tree of Life application was on Jotform. Healing group assessments went out as PDFs by hand and mostly never came back. Post-session surveys were headed for Survey Monkey.
  • We could not tell whether our advertising worked. Meyer Partners was running ads into a site that could not report a conversion back.

What nineteen months of data showed

We exported the complete history before switching — 1 January 2025 to 30 July 2026 — and captured a Google baseline. Three findings mattered:

  • Four pages carry the whole site. Home, Healing Groups, About and Donate were 19,881 of 41,431 pageviews — 48%. Everything else, including 1,400-plus archived devotions, shared the rest.
  • Healing groups is where people actually engage. It is the second most-visited page, and visitors stay three and a half minutes — longer than any other main page and more than twice the homepage. That is people reading carefully because they need it. The new site is built around that.
  • Google shows us to a very large audience that almost never clicks. Over sixteen months Google displayed the site 497,000 times, producing 5,910 clicks. When someone searches our name they click 38% of the time; for everything else it is under 4%. And name searches are under 1% of all impressions — almost everyone Google shows us to is searching for scripture and doctrine, not for our organisation.

What we did about it

  • Archived everything first — all 1,459 pages and every image back to 2013, saved outside Squarespace. Nothing was lost when the subscription ended.
  • Rebuilt the highest-demand content. Pastor Ricky Jacob's seven-part "What Do Lutherans Believe?" series — around 1,500 views, and the reason we get 7,569 impressions a year for "lutheran sacraments" — had been redirecting to the homepage. It is a real page again.
  • Protected our search standing. Every old address now forwards to its correct new home instead of the homepage, which Google treats as a broken page and eventually drops.
  • Brought our own tools in-house — the Tree of Life application, healing group registration with a staff review queue, an events editor staff can use without a developer, and a flyer generator.
  • Made giving work on our own page — the donation form is embedded in our site, tested end to end, with conversion tracking connected so our advertising can finally be measured.

One caution before the next report

Please don't compare the new site's visitor counts directly against the old Squarespace numbers. They are measured differently. Squarespace counted every visitor at the server; our new analytics only counts those who accept the cookie notice that privacy law requires. The new number will read lower even if real traffic grows.

Google Search Console is the honest comparison — it measures the domain, so before and after line up. Watch impressions (should hold), average position (the number to beat is 9.7, up from 13.9), then clicks and click-through rate, which is where the new site should win.

What comes next

  • The devotional archive is our biggest untapped opportunity. Hundreds of thousands of impressions a year point at devotions that currently redirect to the homepage. Rebuilding more of those 1,459 posts is the highest-return work available, and it costs staff time rather than money.
  • Google Ad Grant — nonprofits qualify for up to $10,000 a month in free Google search advertising. Worth pursuing now that the site can receive and measure that traffic.
  • Assessment and intake forms for healing groups, so pre-assessments, waivers and post-session surveys are completed on our site and actually come back — replacing the paid survey tool.
  • Email list — the site now has a signup for the first time. Until this month the only addresses we collected came from people registering for an event.

The short version: LIM had a website that looked fine and did very little. It now has one that carries applications, registrations and giving, that can prove what our advertising produces, and that we own outright. The data says our healing ministry is what draws people most, and that our devotional archive is a far bigger opportunity than anyone realised.
