2. The Google Ad Grant — a decision

Quick recap: Google gives eligible nonprofits up to $10,000 a month in free search ads. These are the text ads at the top of Google results, shown to people who are already searching for things like "trauma healing" or "Christian counseling Fairbanks." Religious organisations qualify as long as they're 501(c)(3). The money is free, but running it is not. Google expects the ads to be looked after and switches off accounts that go stale. The real question is who runs it, not whether to apply.

What another nonprofit just told me. Represent Justice, another nonprofit I work with, has just started on the grant. On 28 September I asked their director, Gordon, what it's really costing them. In short:

  • They hired Nonprofit Megaphone for $5,000 a year. For that, Nonprofit Megaphone gets them approved by Google for Nonprofits, sets up the account, finds the keywords, writes and adjusts the ads, and sends a report every quarter.
  • In Gordon's words: "This is all stuff, frankly, I can do myself. It just is a lot of time… to do it well requires a more hands-on approach than I have the capacity for." And on LIM specifically: "I think your clients should definitely use it. It's a free 10k of advertising, but there's a lot of setup — if they don't have somebody that's versed in digital advertising."
  • He also pointed to a cheaper option: Ad Grants Pilot (Chicago). It uses AI to read your website and write the ads, and a person edits what it produces. Their published price is $199 a month (about $2,400 a year), with a 30-day free trial.
  • Represent Justice is still setting up, so they can't say yet whether it's working. Treat this as what it costs, not what it delivers.

The three ways LIM could run it:

  • Nonprofit Megaphone, about $5,000 a year. Fully hands-off, run by people. It's the biggest firm doing this. Their price isn't published, so $5,000 is what Represent Justice pays.
  • Ad Grants Pilot, about $2,400 a year. The AI does the writing, and I'd check its work for about an hour a month so nothing goes out in the wrong voice for a trauma-healing ministry.
  • Me, from the maintenance block. No extra contract, and I already have two of the hardest pieces in place: the keyword research from this summer, which showed LIM can win searches about trauma healing, and the registration tracking that Google requires and that started working this morning. Setup would take a few days of hours, then a few hours a month.

My recommendation: apply now, whatever we choose to run it. Then start with the Ad Grants Pilot free month and have me review it. If it hasn't produced registrations by January, either I take it over or we move to Nonprofit Megaphone. Honestly, $10,000 a month is a ceiling, not a promise. Searches for healing groups in Alaska are a small pool, and LIM will use a fraction of that. That's why I'd rather not start with the $5,000 option.

The one step only LIM can take: the Google for Nonprofits application. It needs a staff member's Google account on a LIM email address, the EIN, and the organisation details, and Google checks the nonprofit status through a partner called Goodstack. It takes about half an hour to fill in, then days to a couple of weeks to be approved. Jaylene, is this yours, or should Angie do it? I can sit beside whoever does it.
