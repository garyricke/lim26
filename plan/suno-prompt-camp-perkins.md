# Suno prompts — music bed for the Camp Perkins video

For `/camp-perkins`. The reel is **3:01**; the individual cuts are **35s–1:07**.
Everything here is instrumental and written to sit *under* speech, not beside it.

---

## Primary prompt — use this one

**Style / description field:**

```
Warm cinematic acoustic underscore, fingerpicked steel-string guitar, soft felt
piano, sustained warm strings, gentle upright bass, brushed percussion entering
late, hopeful and unhurried, major key, 76 BPM, wide open and spacious, documentary
score, tender not triumphant, sparse arrangement with room to breathe, instrumental
```

**Lyrics field:**

```
[Instrumental]
```

**Structure hint** (paste into lyrics field under `[Instrumental]` if you want the shape):

```
[Intro] sparse guitar and piano, very quiet
[Build] strings enter softly, still restrained
[Middle] brushed percussion, gentle forward motion
[Lift] fuller but never loud
[Outro] returns to solo guitar, resolves warm
```

---

## Alternative — quieter, more reflective

For the individual one-minute cuts, where there's less room.

```
Minimal ambient acoustic bed, solo fingerpicked guitar, soft piano, warm low pad,
no percussion, slow and still, major key, 68 BPM, spacious and reverent, gentle
hope, film underscore, very sparse, instrumental
```

---

## Exclusions — put these in Suno's "Exclude styles" field

```
vocals, choir, drums, drum kit, EDM, synthwave, orchestral hits, epic trailer,
brass swells, tambourine, loud, distorted guitar, worship anthem, key change
```

---

## Mixing it under the voices

- Sit the bed at roughly **-22 to -26 dB** under speech — audible but never competing.
- **Duck it** under every spoken line rather than riding one flat level.
- Let it come up in the gaps between speakers, and at the very end after Emily's
  closing line.
- Fade in over the first 3–4 seconds; don't start cold under the first word.
- Suno tops out around 4 minutes per generation, so the 3:01 reel fits in one.

---

## One caution before generating

**Do not prompt Suno for "Native American" music.** It will produce the generic
wooden-flute-and-tom-tom pastiche, and putting AI-generated imitation Native music
underneath actual Native people speaking would be the exact thing Robert has said
LIM exists to move away from.

The prompts above are deliberately culturally neutral — warm acoustic score that
supports the voices without claiming to represent anyone.

If LIM does want Native music in this piece, the right route is **real musicians**:
commission or license from a Native artist, with credit and payment. Worth asking
Robert or Roberta — LIM almost certainly knows people, and that would be a far
better piece of work than anything generated.
