1 · Email

Three lengths. Use the invitation when you're writing to people directly, the bulletin blurb when you're asking another church to run it for you, and the text message when you're inviting one person from your phone.

The invitation Send to your own list · forwardable

Subject: You're invited — a free afternoon of songs and the stories behind them, Sept 26 in Fairbanks

Hello,

You're invited to Songs & Stories of Hope — a free afternoon of great music and the encouraging stories behind the songs, at Zion Lutheran Church in Fairbanks.

Saturday, September 26 · 2:00–3:30 PM
Zion Lutheran Church, 2982 Davis Road, Fairbanks
Free to attend — no ticket, nothing to sign up for

Becky Key is a singer and songwriter who has spent more than twenty years playing in churches and community halls. She'll sing, and then tell you where each song came from. Her brother, Josh Vigran, is Zion's music director. Pastor Bobby Key brings a message — the honest kind, about where hope actually comes from when life is hard.

Come as you are, bring your family, bring a friend. If you know someone who is carrying something heavy right now, this is an easy afternoon to invite them to.

Everything — the music, the people, a map — is here: lutheranindianministries.org/zion

Hope to see you there.

Posting it for free vs. paying to boost it. Posting costs nothing and reaches the people who already follow LIM and Zion — do that regardless, and ask Zion, Bill and the performers to share it to their own pages, which is where most of the free reach actually comes from. Boosting is the paid version and it's what reaches Fairbanks people who have never heard of either ministry. If Bill wants to spend there, the useful settings are: a radius around Fairbanks that takes in North Pole, adults, no interest targeting (the audience is too small for it to help), and a run of roughly the last two weeks before September 26 with a small daily amount rather than one big push. Facebook will quote the cost before anything is spent. I can set it up and hand it over, or write out the click-by-click for whoever runs the page — whichever Bill prefers.
