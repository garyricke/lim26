What This Is

Some wounds don't heal on their own.

Many of us carry things we've never spoken aloud. Grief that never had a place to go. Harm done to us as children. Pain passed down through generations that we didn't ask for and didn't cause.

The Tree of Life Gathering is three full days set aside for that weight — a place to name it, bring it to the cross, and begin walking out from under it. It is Gospel-centered and Native-led, and it is built on the same conviction that runs through all of our work: you don't have to change to come. God meets you exactly where you are.

Who Should Come

This gathering is for you if…

  • You've experienced or witnessed domestic violence, abuse, or neglect — recently or long ago.
  • You're carrying grief or loss that you've never had space to work through.
  • You feel the weight of historical trauma in your family or your community.
  • You've been through a Healing Group and you're ready to go deeper.
  • You want to walk this road with other Native people who understand it from the inside.

You do not need a faith background. You do not need to have it figured out. You only need to be willing to show up for all three days.

What to Expect

Three days, held carefully.

The gathering runs 8:30 in the morning until 5:30 in the evening each day. Meals are provided. Everything else is built around making it safe to do hard work.

Native-Led

Facilitated by Native people who know the stories, the traditions, the culture, and the humor — and who have walked this road themselves.

Confidential

What is shared in the room stays in the room. Every participant agrees to hold one another's stories in confidence.

At Your Pace

No pressure and no force. You go only where you're ready to go. You come as you are and you leave when the time is right.

How to Come

Applying takes about fifteen minutes.

The Tree of Life Gathering asks more of participants than a weekly Healing Group does, so we ask more up front. The application includes questions about your health, your safety, and your history — not to screen you out, but so our facilitators can prepare well for you and make the room safe for everyone in it.

Answer honestly. Very little disqualifies anyone. If we have concerns, we'll call you and talk it through together.

Your Answers Are Protected

The application asks about difficult things — thoughts of suicide, unsafe relationships, and violence at home. We ask because we want to care for you well, and we treat those answers accordingly.

Your application is read only by the LIM staff involved in this gathering. It is not emailed around, and it is not shared outside our team.

Our facilitators are mandated reporters. If you disclose harm to yourself or another person, we are legally required to report it. We tell you this before you apply so there are no surprises.

If you are in crisis right now, please don't wait on an application. Call or text 988 any time, day or night.

Questions

Before You Apply

What does it cost?

Nothing. There is no cost to attend, and all meals during the gathering are provided. The Tree of Life Gathering is funded by donors who believe Native people should never have to choose between healing and affording it.

Do I have to attend all three days?

Yes. The three days build on one another, and people open up in ways that depend on the group staying whole. If something makes full attendance difficult, tell us on the application — we'd rather talk about it than have you rule yourself out.

Do I need to be Christian?

No. The gathering is rooted in scripture, and we don't hide that. But no faith background is needed and nobody is pressured. You come as you are.

Why does the application ask such personal questions?

Because the gathering goes to hard places, and our facilitators want to be ready to support you there. The questions about safety and mental health help us prepare — they are not a test you can fail.

Will what I share be kept private?

Yes, with one exception we tell everyone up front: our facilitators are mandated reporters and must report disclosures of harm to yourself or others. Everything else stays between you, the group, and the staff supporting the gathering.

What if I've already done a Healing Group?

Many participants have. The gathering is a natural next step for people who have been through Healing the Wounds of Trauma and want to keep going.
