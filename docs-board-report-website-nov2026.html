2 · What visitors read, and where they came from

Read these numbers as proportions, not totals

Google Analytics only counts visitors who click Accept on the site’s cookie notice — a privacy protection we chose deliberately. Our own ad data shows how big the gap is: in the first four days of the Anchorage ad, Facebook delivered 773 visits but only 83 were counted. The counted sample is 449 people, 698 visits and 1,008 page views, and nearly a third of those “people” are overseas bots that stay one second. The patterns below come from the real visitors. 4 · Conversion rate — what we can honestly say

A true conversion rate is actions divided by all visitors, and we don’t yet have the “all visitors” number (see Section 2). The clearest measured case is Anchorage: about 1,000 ad visits produced 6 registrations in the same window, a little under 1%. For a two-day trauma-healing group, a much bigger ask than a click, that is a reasonable start, and it is the first real benchmark LIM has.

5 · What we’re fixing before the next report

  • Count every visit, without cookies. Add a privacy-friendly, server-side visit counter so the board sees full totals, alongside Google Analytics for behaviour. Small monthly cost (about $9).
  • Track the newer pages. Camp Perkins, the event recaps, the event flyers and the Tree of Life pages were built after launch and aren’t yet counted at all.
  • Report registrations as a goal in Google Analytics, so “did it work” is answered automatically each month.
  • Restore Google Search Console access, which shows how often LIM appears in Google searches and for what.
  • Connect newsletter sign-ups to MailChimp and make the sign-up more visible — one sign-up in two months says it’s too easy to miss.
